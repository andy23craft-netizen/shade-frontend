# Home Projects — Master Roadmap

> **Purpose:** Break the larger vision for the house into distinct, achievable projects. Each project should have its own finish line rather than feeling like one endless renovation.
>
> Projects are arranged roughly from **smallest / least expensive / least disruptive** to **largest / most expensive / most involved**. Material lists are intentionally preliminary. Each project can later be expanded into its own detailed plan, measurements, budget, shopping list, and build sequence.

---

# 4. Full-Length Mirror / Hidden Hallway Closet Door

**Project Size:** Medium  
**Cost:** $$  
**Labor:** One weekend, potentially two depending on finish work  
**Disruption:** Low–Medium

## Goal

Remove the existing cabinet/closet doors at the end of the hallway and replace them with one large finished panel carrying a full-length mirror, disguising the storage behind it.

The finished piece should read visually as a large hallway mirror rather than a closet door.

## Materials to Price

### Door

- [ ] Cabinet-grade plywood
- [ ] Hardwood edging or edge banding
- [ ] Wood filler
- [ ] Primer
- [ ] Paint / stain / clear finish

### Mirror

- [ ] Full-length mirror
- [ ] Mirror adhesive
- [ ] Mirror clips / mechanical retention if appropriate
- [ ] Optional trim or frame around mirror

### Hardware

- [ ] Heavy-duty concealed hinges / pivot hardware
- [ ] Magnetic catches
- [ ] Push-to-open latch or concealed handle
- [ ] Structural screws
- [ ] Shims
- [ ] Stops / bumpers

## Important Design Considerations

A full sheet of plywood plus a large mirror can become **very heavy**. Hardware selection and attachment to framing will be a major part of the detailed project plan.

The eventual plan should determine:

- Finished door weight
- Hinge/pivot capacity
- Wall/framing attachment
- Swing clearance
- Floor clearance
- Mirror thickness and weight
- How the door is opened without revealing that it is a door
- Whether the existing cabinet face needs reinforcement

## Milestone

**The hallway ends at a full-length mirror—and the storage behind it effectively disappears.**

# Suggested Project Rhythm

The goal is **not to finish the house as quickly as possible**. The goal is to keep making visible progress without allowing one enormous project to consume every available weekend, dollar, and ounce of enthusiasm.

A useful rhythm is:

**Quick Win → Room Improvement → Quick Win → Medium Build → Major Project**

Small projects provide frequent completion milestones. Medium projects create noticeable transformations. Large projects remain exciting because they are something being deliberately worked toward rather than something that must be completed before anything else matters.

The **living-room library should remain the horizon project**. It does not need to monopolize the schedule immediately. Measuring it, designing it, pricing the ladder system, building a prototype shelf section, purchasing materials, constructing the lower cabinets, and eventually installing the upper shelves can each become satisfying milestones of their own.

Likewise, the **walk-in closet and hidden emergency area should eventually become one coordinated renovation plan**, even though they remain separate goals on this roadmap. Designing them together avoids reopening finished walls later.

As each project approaches its turn, spin this master entry into a dedicated project document containing:

1. Exact measurements
2. Finished design
3. Construction drawings / layout
4. Detailed material quantities
5. Tool requirements
6. Budget
7. Build sequence
8. Safety / code / permit considerations
9. Shopping list
10. Individual work-session milestones
11. Final punch list
